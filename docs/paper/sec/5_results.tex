\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We find that all transformations except for \verb|Mirroring| suffer performance loss of 5 to 95 percentage points of the range between the real distribution and the center bias on average, depending on model and transformation type. This holds true for both the NSS and IG metrics. While both models perform better than the center bias for untransformed images on average, they perform worse than the center bias for many transformations. For similar transformations at differing intensities (\eg \verb|ContrastChange_1| and \verb|ContrastChange_2|), an increase in the intensity of the transformation leads to a loss in prediction accuracy. \Cref{nss} and \Cref{ig} plot each model's NSS and IG scores respectively.

These results confirm our hypothesis that most digital transformations will degrade the model's prediction accuracy. However, for the specific case of the \verb|Mirroring| transformation, the model's prediction accuracy appears to be unaffected. It is likely that models will require additional training on data collected for transformed images (excluding \verb|Mirroring|) in order to mitigate performance loss.

\subsection{Loss indicator results}

Of the ten pairs of independent and dependent variables outlined previously, we find no pairs where plots for both DeepGaze IIE \cite{deepgazeiie} and UNISAL \cite{unisal} exhibit a correlation coefficient above $0.5$, which is our threshold for useful indicators. If we define "correlation consensus" to be the minimum of the correlation coefficients of the two models for any given pair of variables on any given transformation, the maximum of all correlation consensus values we found was $0.39$.

In the absense of strong loss indicators, gathering gaze distribution data from human trials is required in order to check performance loss for arbitrary transformations.
